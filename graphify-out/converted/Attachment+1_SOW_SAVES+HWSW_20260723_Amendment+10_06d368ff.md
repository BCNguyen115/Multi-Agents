# OVERVIEW

Pursuant to the Federal Information Technology Acquisition Reform Act (FITARA) and the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) for category management, the consolidation and centralization of IT equipment focuses on eliminating redundancies, increasing efficiency, and delivering more value and savings.  To help achieve these goals, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) Lines of Business (LOB), Staff Offices (SOs), Regions, and Centers will utilize our mandatory use contract under our Acquisition Management System (AMS 3.8.6) called the Strategic Sourcing for the Acquisition of Various Equipment and Supplies (SAVES).  SAVES contracts provide the FAA with a simplified process for obtaining commonly used products and services at prices associated with volume buying, while maintaining or improving the quality of purchases and vendors’ service levels.

Additionally, in accordance with the FAA Information Security and Privacy Program & Policy (1370.121B), the FAA must consolidate procurements for commodity items such as laptops, desktops, software and licensing, and other hardware and components. In accordance with Section B, the portfolio of products available will be a combination of hardware and software which will be designated as "standard products" by the FAA’s Technology Control Board (TCB), the Enterprise Software Board (ESB) and any comparably authorized group within the Department of Transportation (DOT). These products must adhere to established configuration management criteria throughout its life cycle.

# SCOPE

This SAVES Statement of Work (SOW) for Commercial Products (Hardware (HW) and Software (SW)) is a DOT-wide strategic sourcing contract administered and managed by the FAA Offices of Acquisition and Business Services (ACQ) and Information and Technology Services (AIT). The products and services included in this SOW include, but are not limited to, software, network infrastructure components (servers, switches, storage arrays, and network devices), personal computing devices (workstations, laptops, tablets, thin clients, notebooks, and similar items), computer peripherals and accessories, audio/visual equipment, and miscellaneous equipment (large screens, touch screens, scanners, projectors, desktop printers, multi-functional devices (MFD)), software to support the above mentioned hardware, and cloud based services by various Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEM) that meet the agency's diverse business requirements. Additionally, the Contractor may provide product-based services based on the business requirement. These services must be directly related to the range of products offered under the Contract and must be performed as requested by the Contracting Officer on individual delivery orders. Lastly, the Contractor must facilitate the acquisition, deployment and maintenance of IT assets under this contract by   providing services including, but not limited to, asset recovery/disposition and warehousing.

This SOW DOES NOT include: non-COTS software items, software development for COTS items, web development and web design services, software operations and maintenance (O&M) services, alterations to a physical site (e.g., wiring, electrical, plumbing, construction), level of effort (LOE) support services including full time equivalent employees with a yearly period of performance (i.e. option years).

# PERSONAL COMPUTING DEVICES – CATEGORY 1 AND 2

The requirements include portable and non-portable personal computing devices including, but not limited to: workstations, desktops, thin clients, laptops, notebooks, tablets, and similar products. These requirements may also include components that directly support complete product solutions, including, but not limited to, hard disk and optical drives, memory modules and upgrades, web cameras, video cards, computer cases, network interface cards, port replicators, docking stations, glare screens, multimedia devices, interface adapter cards, expansion bays, processor and motherboard upgrades, keyboards, mice, accessories, identification/authentication devices, portable storage devices, memory and graphics cards, speakers, expansion chassis, warranties, extended warranties, maintenance, and software licenses.

When the purchase of computers includes peripherals like a keyboard, mouse, docking station and other accessories, each computer must be packed in a box with one (1) each of the required peripherals and shipped to the FAA as a complete kit.  FAA issued barcodes must be physically affixed to the top of the computer (bottom right corner of the laptop lid or top front edge of the desktop) and the last nine (9) digits of the FAA barcode must be added to the asset identification field in the bios before shipping. All computing equipment must be shipped with a tamper-evident indicator on the outside packaging.

All personal computing devices must include a warranty of not less than three (3) years unless the purchasing official (i.e., Contracting Officer or purchase card holder) specifies a variation to this requirement. The warranty for computing devices must include, but is not limited to, all of the main internal components contained within (e.g., optical drive, hard drive, memory, mother boards, batteries, and screens) and external components such as docking stations and stylus’. All Contractor provided quotes must explicitly state which personal computing device components are not covered by the three (3) year warranty. Additionally, the quotes must include what warranty is included for those items. Under no circumstances must the Government be required to ship a computing device to any contractor, OEM, or other facility for the purpose of warranty repair. If a computing device is to be replaced under warranty, the Contractor must provide, at no cost to the Government, an advance replacement, and a shipping label for the return of the defective item. The Government will return the defective item to the Contractor within 30 days of receiving the advance replacement, using the packaging materials from the advance replacement and the shipping label. The Government must retain all nonvolatile memory devices, including but not limited to hard disk drives and solid-state drives, even if the non-volatile memory device is the defective part.

For Hardware Support Services/Tag Transfer of Dell Products, the Contractor must transfer title of Dell Products and the asset/service identification number (e.g. the Service Tag or Asset Number) to the End-User that is associated with the Dell Products the Contractor have purchased for resale in accordance with Dell.  The process to complete such transfer may be found at www.support.dell.com, which process Dell may change from time to time. It is Contractor’s responsibility to ensure compliance with the latest version of the posted process. The Contractor acknowledges that their failure to properly transfer the asset/service identification number of the Dell Products will result in the End-User's inability to receive hardware support services from Dell for such Dell Products, and Dell will not be liable to the Contractor or to any End-User for any such failure. In the event a designated end-user seeks warranty or other service from Dell that requires evidence of ownership, and the Contractor has not yet accomplished such ownership transfer, the Contractor hereby expressly authorizes Dell to immediately affect ownership transfer.
